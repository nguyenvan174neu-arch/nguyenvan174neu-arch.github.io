When Dreams Find Their Way to You

A reflection on dreams, passion, PhD life, and the unexpected ways opportunities sometimes find us.
September 2026 · Van Nguyen Thi

Today, as I registered for this year’s PhD Colloquium, I suddenly realised that almost a year has passed.

It took me back to this same time last year, when two very different things were happening in my life almost simultaneously.

One belonged to my PhD journey.

The other belonged to a very personal dream.

Looking back, both remind me of something I have always wanted to believe:

Have a dream, and keep pursuing it with determination. Sometimes, the universe will find a way to help you with the rest.

When Something Good Happens When You Least Expect It

Last year’s PhD Colloquium came during one of the busiest periods of my PhD. I did not have particularly high expectations. I simply thought: I have registered, so I will prepare as well as I can and give it my best.

After my presentation, I walked away wondering how much of my finance research had really connected with an audience largely outside my field.

Most of the panel members that day came from Marketing or Information Technology. From some of the questions and comments, I had the feeling that the technical details of my research might not have been particularly easy for people outside Finance to follow.

So any kind of recognition was the last thing I expected.

To my surprise, my paper was selected as one of the five best papers at the PhD Colloquium that year.

I still remember how unexpected that moment felt.

But there is something else I remember even more clearly than the result.

Throughout my presentation, everyone was listening incredibly carefully.

They might not have fully understood every model, method, or detail behind the research. But they were paying attention. They wanted to listen.

And that made me wonder:

What makes people stay engaged with your presentation even when they may not fully understand everything you are talking about?

Perhaps it is not always just about the content.

Perhaps it is also about energy.

It is about how deeply you believe in what you are doing. It is the passion you put into something you have created. It is in your eyes, your voice, and the excitement of someone who has spent countless hours living with a research question and genuinely believes that the question matters.

Some things do not need to be explained explicitly. People can simply feel them.

Passion is contagious.

Another Dream, on Another Stage

Around the same time, a completely different story was unfolding.

A year earlier, when I had been living in Wellington for about a year, I attended the annual music show organised by the Vietnamese student community in Wellington for the first time.

I was simply a member of the audience, sitting below the stage.

But as I watched everyone perform, one very clear thought came into my mind:

“One day, I will stand on that stage too.”

There was no specific plan.

I did not know when.

I did not know how.

I simply wanted to be up there and sing.

Then, one year later, when I saw the casting announcement for the show, I signed up almost immediately.

That small dream became a reality in a way that was even more exciting than I had imagined.

I was not performing in just one act, but three, taking on different roles as both a singer and a dancer. I also had the opportunity to experiment with very different styles of music — from a gentle pop song to chầu văn, a distinctive form of traditional Vietnamese music.

For the pop performance, I even suggested adding some rock elements to make the arrangement more energetic and distinctive.

I remember being so excited that even though I had a sore throat at the time, I still did not want to stop practising.

And then I had a moment when I suddenly realised:

One year earlier, I had been sitting below that stage thinking:

“I want to be up there.”

One year later, I actually was.

Perhaps “The Universe” Is Not Entirely About Magic

When I say, “the universe will help you make your dreams come true,” I do not mean that we can simply sit still and wait for something magical to happen.

Perhaps the mechanism is much more practical than that.

When you genuinely want something, you begin to notice opportunities connected to it.

A casting announcement that someone else might scroll past immediately catches your attention.

A call for papers makes you think, Why not give it a try?

An opportunity appears, and instead of asking yourself, “Am I good enough?”, you think:

“Let me try.”

Dreams make us pay attention.

Persistence makes us act.

And action gives the things that once existed only in our imagination a chance to become real.

Perhaps that is how “the universe” actually works.

But What About the PhD?

There was a question I sometimes asked myself:

Dreams or PhD life — which should come first?

Over time, I realised that perhaps I do not need to choose.

A PhD is certainly demanding. There are days filled with regressions, revisions, deadlines, unexpected results, and research questions that seem to have no final answer.

But perhaps that is exactly why making time for the things I love matters so much.

Singing.

Dancing.

Learning a new language.

For me, these things do not take time away from my PhD.

They give energy back to my PhD.

The moments when I get to live with my passions feel like recharging a battery. I return to my desk with a refreshed mind, more energy, and sometimes even new ideas.

For me, work–life balance does not necessarily mean dividing life into two completely separate halves.

Perhaps it simply means:

Do not let one part of your life become so big that all the other parts of who you are begin to disappear.

A Little Corner for the Things We Love

Another thing this musical journey gave me was the opportunity to connect with many more Vietnamese people in Wellington.

What I found fascinating was that most of us were not professional musicians.

Everyone came from a different background. Everyone had their own work, studies, responsibilities, and personal story.

But once we entered the rehearsal room, all those titles seemed to disappear.

We were simply people who loved music.

And I realised something I really admired about the people around me:

No matter how busy life became, they still kept a little corner of their hearts for the things they loved.

A place where music could continue to exist.

A place where passion could continue to grow.

A place that reminded them that they were more than their jobs, degrees, or everyday responsibilities.

I think there is something beautiful about that.

Perhaps one of the most interesting ways to understand the world is also to surround ourselves with people whose lives are different from our own. Everyone carries a story, a perspective, and a world that we may know nothing about until we take the time to listen.

And despite all those differences, passion has an extraordinary way of bringing people together.

One Year Later

And today, almost one year later, I find myself sitting in front of my screen, registering for the PhD Colloquium again.

But I am no longer exactly the same person I was a year ago.

Another year has brought more research, more presentations, more moments standing in front of audiences, some successes, some disappointments, new experiences, and many things I could never have predicted.

Looking back at those two stories that unfolded around the same time — a best-paper recognition that I never expected and a stage that I had once only dreamed of standing on — I believe even more strongly in one thing:

Not every dream comes true.

And simply wanting something badly enough does not mean that it will magically happen.

But having a dream that is clear enough helps you know what you are looking for.

Having a passion strong enough helps you recognise an opportunity when it appears.

And having enough persistence gives you the courage to step forward when that door opens.

So keep dreaming.

Whether your dreams are big or small.

Whether they are about your career, a stage, a journey, or simply the person you hope to become.

Dream it.
Believe in it.
Work for it.

And perhaps, one day, you will look back and quietly realise:

The dream came true.
