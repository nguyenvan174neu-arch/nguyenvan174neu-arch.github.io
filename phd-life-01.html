Curious Enough to Begin

Curiosity, new places, new people, and the unexpected things a PhD teaches us beyond research.
September 2026 · Van Nguyen Thi
I started a PhD thinking I was signing up to become a researcher. Apparently, the package also includes learning about people, courage, uncertainty, and myself — none of which appeared in the programme description. 😅 Welcome to my PhD Life series.

Before starting my PhD, I thought I had a fairly clear idea of what it would be like.

I knew it would be challenging. There would be endless papers to read, research questions to figure out, data to work with, regressions to run, and probably many moments of wondering whether I actually knew what I was doing. 😅

But more than being worried, I was curious and excited.

I was excited about the things I did not know yet.

I wondered what new ideas I would discover, what places I would explore, and what kind of people I would meet along the way.

Moving abroad made that curiosity even stronger. I was not simply starting a PhD; I was also beginning a new life in a country with a culture very different from the one I grew up in.

And then I arrived in New Zealand and realised that I was not adapting to just one new culture.

I was suddenly surrounded by people from many different backgrounds, each bringing their own perspectives and ways of seeing the world. It made the experience feel like a much bigger classroom than I had expected. 🌏

And honestly, I quite like that.

Then, of course, there is the actual PhD.

Read papers. Find a research question. Collect data. Run regressions. Write papers. Present them. Revise them.

Sounds quite straightforward.

That was before I discovered that a regression can look absolutely beautiful one evening and, after adding one control variable the next morning, suddenly behave as if it has never met you before. 🤔

As my PhD journey has unfolded, I have realised that the research appearing in my thesis is only part of what I am learning.

Some lessons come from papers and supervisors.

Others come from conversations, unexpected experiences, mistakes, difficult decisions — and from the people I meet along the way.

One thing I have particularly come to appreciate is that being independent does not mean doing everything alone.

I have been fortunate to have people around me who introduce me to new technologies, research tools, and ideas that help me work smarter and think differently about my academic journey.

And quite often, those conversations leave me thinking:

“How did I not know about this before?”

I am genuinely grateful for that.

It reminds me that independence does not mean knowing everything yourself. It also means being curious enough to learn from others, open enough to try something unfamiliar, and then figuring out how to make that knowledge your own.

Good people do not walk the road for us. But sometimes they point out a path that we did not even know was there.

Perhaps that brings me back to the curiosity I had before starting the PhD.

I still do not know exactly where this journey will take me.

And I definitely do not know what my next regression will decide to do. 😅

But maybe not knowing is part of what makes the journey interesting.

I started the PhD curious about the research I would do, the places I would discover, and the people I would meet.

Along the way, I am discovering something else too:

Sometimes the most interesting thing about exploring a new world is discovering a new version of yourself within it.
